Fit check is when you film yourself showing off your outfit and pretend you're not fishing for compliments. The format is simple. Stand in front of a mirror, hit a pose or do a little spin, and let the clothes speak. Sometimes there's a voiceover explaining where each piece is from. Sometimes it's just the fit and a trending sound.

The phrase has been around since at least 2017 in streetwear circles, but TikTok turned it into a standardized content format. Before the app, people posted "outfit of the day" photos on Instagram. Fit check is the video version, and it's way more performative.

## How to use fit check without sounding like a bot

There's an unspoken rule. If you're actually dressed well, you don't need to say "fit check." You just post it. Saying "fit check" out loud in the video is a subtle admission that you're not sure about the outfit. It's like asking "does this look good?" without actually asking.

The word "fit" is doing specific work here too. On TikTok, "fit" means outfit. But "fit check" implies a request for validation that you're pretending isn't a request. Just saying "my fit" is a statement. "Fit check" is a question wearing a statement's clothes.

The best fit checks don't use the phrase at all. They just show the fit. The worst ones are clearly just an excuse to show off a new purchase. Honestly? Just post the outfit and go. You'll know the difference immediately.

**DO:** Film it in natural light, keep it under 10 seconds, let the outfit breathe.
**DON'T:** Explain every brand name, do a 30-second transition, caption it "humble fit check" when you're wearing $2,000 of designer.
