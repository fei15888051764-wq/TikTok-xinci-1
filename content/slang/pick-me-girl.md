A pick me girl markets herself to men by distancing herself from other women. She'll say "girls are too much drama, I only hang with guys," brag about never wearing makeup while wearing makeup, and treat your boyfriend like a co-host. The goal is getting picked. Hence the name.

I first noticed the term in spring 2021, when Kelsey Jensen's "Chill Girl" POV series kept landing on my FYP. She'd play the girl who only drinks beer, hits you with "I'm not tired, why are you out of breath" halfway up a hike, then pulls your boyfriend into a photo real quick. Every comment section said the same word. Pick me.

## It started way before TikTok

Most people assume TikTok invented this. Nope.

The spiritual prequel is a 2005 episode of Grey's Anatomy (season 2), where Meredith begs Derek: "Pick me. Choose me. Love me." Ellen Pompeo pushed back hard on that scene, hating that her character begged a man on screen; years later she joked that her daughter's friends now throw "pick me" around as a straight-up insult. The beg became the burn.

The actual coinage happened on Black Twitter. On February 28, 2016, @_PettyCrocker amplified a hashtag proposed by @itsonlytwiterr: #TweetLikeAPickMe. The tweets roasted women auditioning for male approval, the tag was trending within days, and Blavity covered it on March 1. A second wave hit in 2018. Wiktionary even traces "pick me" girls in print back to 2013, so the idea predates the hashtag.

Urban Dictionary's top definition, sitting at 3,800+ upvotes as of May 2020, calls her "a girl who seeks male validation by indirectly or directly insinuating that she is 'not like the other girls,'" then lands the killing blow: "Basically a female version of a simp."

## A quick timeline

- **2005:** Grey's Anatomy airs "Pick me. Choose me. Love me." Meredith's monologue becomes the accidental prototype.
- **Feb 28, 2016:** #TweetLikeAPickMe launches on Black Twitter.
- **Mar 1, 2016:** Blavity covers the trending tag.
- **2018:** second Twitter wave.
- **May 2020:** UD's top definition passes 3,800 upvotes.
- **Mar 3, 2021:** @hannah.montoya posts a skit about getting the pick me girl as your group project partner. It pulls 9.9 million views and basically installs the character on TikTok.
- **2021 to 2024:** videos under the pick me hashtags rack up 2 billion+ views combined.
- **2025:** Sabrina Carpenter's album Man's Best Friend gets labeled "pick-me poetry," and the fight over the word's limits goes mainstream.

## The word got too big for itself

The original target was narrow: a girl performing low-maintenance coolness while stepping on other women. Specific behavior, specific vibe. Then the word got popular, and popular words get lazy.

By 2025 you had listeners calling Sabrina Carpenter's Man's Best Friend "pick-me poetry" because the album openly wants male attention. Cosmopolitan pushed back, arguing the label had stretched to the point where any expression of romantic desire toward men counts as pick me, which drains the word of its actual meaning. Which, fair.

My take: the word was sharper when it described a strategy instead of a gender. You can want a boyfriend without being a pick me. The tell was never wanting men. It was kneecapping other women to get one.

Scroll the comments under any video of a girl saying she'd rather watch football with the guys and you'll find "pick me behavior" and "let her live" fighting it out reply by reply.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does pick me girl mean on TikTok?

On TikTok, a pick me girl is a stock character: the girl who insists she's "not like other girls," puts other women down, and performs chillness for male approval. She shows up in skits and in comments calling out that exact energy in real people.

### Is a pick me girl just a female simp?

Close, not identical. UD's top definition calls her "a female version of a simp," but the mechanics differ: a simp worships the person he likes, while a pick me disparages the competition. Simping is about the target. Being a pick me is about the rivals.

### Is the word overused at this point?

Honestly, yes. The Carpenter debate showed it now gets thrown at women for simply liking men. Aimed at the actual behavior (self-promotion via putting other women down) it's still precise. Aimed at any woman with a crush, it's noise.

## Pronunciation

"PICK-mee girl," stress on the first syllable. Say it exactly like Meredith begged it in 2005.
