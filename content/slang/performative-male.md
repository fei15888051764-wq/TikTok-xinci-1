A performative male is a guy whose personality feels rehearsed for an audience: the female gaze or the timeline. Know Your Meme defines the type as behavior that seems "inauthentic," revolving around "performing" for other people's tastes. Merriam-Webster's slang dictionary is blunter: he engages insincerely to appear safe, sophisticated, or feminist.

You've seen this man. Matcha latte in one hand, Simone de Beauvoir in the other. Clairo through wired headphones. A Labubu clipped to a printed tote. None of that is illegal; the accusation is that it's a costume.

## The summer of the matcha man

The term started trending on Google in July 2025, and Canadian outlets (CTV, CP24) were already running explainers. By September, US campuses were hosting "performative male contests," students of every gender dressing as the archetype for gift cards or books.

The contest details beat any dictionary. Cornell's student paper reported a contestant with a note pinned to his shirt: "I hate period cramps (6' 2" btw)." At UW-Madison, William & Mary, and UConn, students threw pads into the crowd. Subtle it was not.

Every decade gets this guy. The 2000s had emo boys, the 2010s had indie soft bois, and the 2020s got a man whose tote bag is a thesis statement.

## How to spot one (and when not to)

University of Toronto professor Kate Maddalena told CP24 the jokes work as pretty substantive social criticism, and the meme's already been reappropriated: it's a Halloween costume now, and plenty of men are in on it. Her worry: guys now scared to read feminist books in public at all. Her test: "if you're actually reading it, it isn't performing, it's honest."

That line is the whole game, honestly. Reading Beauvoir on the train because you want to is fine. Angling the cover at the carriage is not.

## The do's and don'ts

- **DO** use it for behavior, not objects. Matcha is a drink. The performance is the caption about the drink.
- **DO** clock the taste that changes with the audience. Beauvoir on the grid, something else in the group chat.
- **DON'T** tag every guy with niche hobbies. Sometimes a tote is just a tote.
- **DON'T** use it on someone who can actually discuss the book. He passes.
- **DO** remember the contests exist because everyone, contestants included, can tell the difference.
