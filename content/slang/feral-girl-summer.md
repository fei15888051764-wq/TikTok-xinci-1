Feral girl summer is when you stop trying. The skincare routine becomes splashing water on your face. The outfit becomes whatever was on the floor. The diet becomes "whatever's in the fridge." It's the TikTok trend that turned being a mess into a movement, and honestly? It was overdue.

I first saw this term in early 2022 under a TikTok where a woman filmed herself eating cold pizza in bed at 2 PM on a Tuesday, still in yesterday's clothes, with the caption "feral girl summer is here." The comments were a mix of concern and envy. That video got millions of views. Everyone was tired of being polished.

## Where feral girl summer came from

The phrase emerged as a direct reaction to the "that girl" aesthetic that dominated TikTok in 2021. "That girl" woke up at 5 AM, did yoga, drank green juice, journaled, and had her life together by 8 AM. She was aspirational, organized, and completely exhausting to watch.

Feral girl summer said no to all of it. Around early 2022, creators started posting videos of themselves doing the opposite. Eating cereal for dinner. Wearing the same hoodie three days in a row. Not brushing their hair and calling it a "texture moment." The hashtag #feralgirl got hundreds of millions of views by mid-2022.

The trend accelerated when celebrities like Florence Pugh and Julia Fox embraced it, pushing the movement from internet niche to mainstream acceptance.

## How people actually use it

On TikTok, the usage is celebratory. Someone posts a video of themselves with smudged mascara, eating chips in bed, and the comments are "feral girl summer energy." It's not an insult. It's a badge of honor. The messier, the better.

The trend also has specific visual markers. Unbrushed hair. No makeup or deliberately messy makeup. Oversized clothes. Food stains that aren't accidental. The aesthetic is anti-aesthetic, which makes it immediately recognizable.

There's a seasonal aspect too. "Feral girl summer" implies a temporary state. You can be feral in summer and then return to civilization in fall. That temporariness makes it feel less like a lifestyle and more like a vacation from expectations.

## Feral girl summer vs goblin mode: what's the actual difference?

Goblin mode, which went viral around the same time, is more extreme. Goblin mode means completely abandoning social norms. Feral girl summer is just messy. You still go outside. You still see people. You just don't bother with the performance.

Goblin mode was named Oxford Word of the Year 2022, which tells you something about the cultural moment. Both trends emerged from the same post-2020 exhaustion. After years of isolation and online performance, people were done trying to look good for an audience.

The difference is also in gender coding. Goblin mode is gender-neutral. Feral girl summer is specifically feminine, a rejection of the beauty standards that demand women be constantly presentable. The gender specificity matters.

## Why it blew up

The pandemic created a generation of people who stopped performing for others. When you spent two years in sweatpants, going back to structured outfits felt absurd. Feral girl summer gave language to that reluctance.

TikTok's algorithm also helped. The platform rewards authenticity, and nothing is more authentic than admitting you look like a disaster. The contrast with the polished "that girl" content made feral girl videos stand out immediately.

Honestly? I think the trend works because it's sustainable. Being "that girl" requires money, time, and energy most people don't have. Being feral requires nothing. That's the whole appeal.
