Pookie is what you call your partner when you've run out of normal pet names. It's a term of endearment that blew up on TikTok around 2023, mostly through couple accounts where one person films the other doing literally anything and captions it "look at my pookie." The word itself has existed for decades, but TikTok turned it into a specific kind of cringe-cute affection.

I first saw this under a video where a guy recorded his girlfriend making cereal at 2am. The caption was just "my pookie 🥺" and it had 4 million views. That was early 2023. By mid-year, every couple account had adopted it. The appeal is that it sounds intentionally childish. You're not saying "my love." You're saying something that sounds like a cartoon character.

## Where did this even come from?

The word "pookie" has been around since at least the 1970s as a generic nickname. It shows up in old sitcoms and baby name forums. But the TikTok version is different. It got its current meaning from couple content creators who needed a pet name that felt more performative than "babe."

The real explosion happened when TikTok's algorithm started pushing couple content hard in 2022-2023. Creators realized that using a slightly absurd pet name got more comments than normal ones. "Pookie" hit the sweet spot of being recognizable but not overused yet. By late 2023, it was unavoidable.

The specific TikTok format matters here. A "my pookie" video isn't just any couple content. It follows a pattern. Soft lighting. A mundane activity. A voiceover that's barely above a whisper. And always, always, the word "pookie" delivered with intentional cringe. If you swap in "babe," the video loses its entire comedic engine.

## Pookie vs Babe: Why TikTok Chose the Weird One

| | Pookie | Babe |
|---|---|---|
| **Vibe** | Cringe-cute, performative | Normal, expected |
| **Comments** | "Protect pookie" / "pookie privilege" | "Couple goals" (generic) |
| **TikTok era** | 2023+ | 2010s Instagram |
| **Energy** | Intentionally childish | Adult relationship |

The framework is simple: if you call someone "babe," you are in a relationship. If you call them "pookie," you are in a relationship and you want the algorithm to know about it.

## How people actually use it

It's almost always possessive. "My pookie." Not just "pookie." The "my" is doing a lot of work here. It marks territory in the softest possible way.

You'll see it in comments too. Someone posts a video of their partner and strangers will comment "protect pookie at all costs" or "pookie privilege." It's become a community thing, not just a couple thing. "Pookie privilege" refers to the unfair advantage of being someone's pookie. Getting away with things because you're cute. "He let his pookie skip the line" is a real comment I've seen under videos. Try substituting "girlfriend" there. It doesn't land.

> @username: bro called her pookie in front of the boys i can't breathe
> @reply: that's commitment right there

The word has also spawned a minor backlash. Some people find it infantilizing. "Why are grown adults calling each other pookie?" is a common comment under these videos. Which, fair. But the people using it clearly don't care.
