"Aura farming" is what happens when Gen Alpha discovers spirituality and immediately gamifies it. The term describes performing calculated, camera-ready behaviors to boost your perceived social or spiritual energy for content creation and online validation. Actually having good vibes is not the point. Looking like you do is. You will see it in videos where someone explains that drinking lemon water at 6am "raises your frequency" in a way that feels more like set dressing than actual belief. Nobody in the comments agrees on whether it is inspiring or pathetic.

## Why everyone started farming

The phrase blew up in late 2024 when creators posted "aura checks" where they rated their own spiritual energy on a scale of 1 to 10 based on mundane activities. Brushing your teeth? Plus two aura points. Checking your ex's Instagram? Minus five. It was stupid and addictive. By 2025, "aura farming" became the critique of that behavior, describing anyone who treats personal growth like a content strategy designed to harvest engagement.

I saw a TikTok in March where a girl filmed herself "cleansing her aura" by buying an overpriced smoothie and walking through a park slowly. The video went viral. The top comment: "this is just farming at this point." Exactly. I watched a creator last month explain that reorganizing his bookshelf "shifted his energy," and the comments were split between people asking for the book list and people calling it obvious aura farming.

> @username: this is just farming at this point 💀

A girl buys a smoothie and walks through a park slowly for a TikTok. Top comment: "aura farming at its finest." That is the accusation. That is the word in action.

## The backlash

Not everyone buys it. The pushback is basically repackaged narcissism with a wellness filter. Critics point out that turning your mental health into a performance creates pressure to always be "vibing high," which is just another form of anxiety wrapped in sage and good lighting. You cannot have a bad day anymore. Honestly? This one annoys me. There is nothing wrong with crystals or smoothies. The problem is when you need an audience for your healing. That is not healing. That is content.
