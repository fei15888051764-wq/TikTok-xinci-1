"Ohio" used to be a state. Now it is an adjective. On TikTok, calling something "Ohio" means it is awkward, bizarre, or inexplicably weird in a way that defies logic. The word comes from the "Only in Ohio" meme that started on Twitter around 2019, where people posted photos of strange occurrences and blamed them on the Buckeye State. A deer standing in a Walmart aisle? Ohio. By 2024, the meme had compressed into a single word. Now you can just say "that is so Ohio" and everyone knows what you mean.

## A quick timeline

- 2019: "Only in Ohio" starts on Twitter as a caption for weird news stories.
- 2021: The meme jumps to TikTok. Creators make compilations of strange Ohio headlines.
- 2022: The phrase shortens. People start saying "Ohio moment" or just "Ohio."
- 2023: Gen Alpha adopts it as a general adjective for anything cringe or unhinged.
- 2024: The word detaches from the state entirely. A weird video from Florida gets called Ohio.
- 2026: It is just vocabulary. "Ohio" means weird. The state is collateral damage.

## How to use it

You do not need to have been to Ohio. You do not even need to know where it is. You just need to recognize the vibe. Someone trips over nothing? Ohio. A raccoon washing cotton candy until it dissolves? Extremely Ohio. The word captures any situation where reality seems to have glitched.

I used it last week when my roommate tried to make pasta by boiling it in a microwave. He looked at me like I had insulted his ancestors. I just said "Ohio behavior." He knew.
